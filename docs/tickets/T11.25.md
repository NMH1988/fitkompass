### T11.25 (P2) A comment beside a recipe's stars, and the coach's marks on Today (M) · status: todo · issue NMH1988/fitkompass#62
**Serves:** Capstone (`docs/BRIEF_CAPSTONE.md`): E1b (the gymer tells the coach why, not only how many stars; Today says where an entry came from), E3 (who reads a free-text comment, and what it may give away, decided and said on the page); beyond the brief (owner's request, 2026-11-28) for the feature itself; Sprint 3 (`docs/BRIEF_SPRINT3.md`): none; Sprint 2 (`docs/BRIEF.md`): none.
**Depends on:** T11.8; T11.9 (order only: it offers the rating on the Meal plan page and takes the next migration number); T11.18 (order only, part B: both change how Today lists entries)
**Acceptance criteria** (proposed by Claude and approved by the owner, 2026-11-28, with the owner's decisions in the Notes)

*Part A: a comment beside the stars*
- [ ] A gymer who may rate a recipe (T11.8's check: their current coach's recipe, and they logged its dish) may add a comment in their own words beside their stars, at most `config.RATING_COMMENT_MAX_CHARS` characters (proposed 300), trimmed, on the Recipes page and wherever T11.9 offers the rating; a comment needs a rating of the same recipe first (decision 7); a blank comment is no comment; the store refuses a comment too long or of the wrong type with an error that names the field, never the value, and every case where the gymer may not rate with the same `NotFound`, writing nothing - store tests in the refused-cases matrix of `tests/test_store_access.py`.
- [ ] A comment is a row of its own (a new table, e.g. `recipe_comments`: the recipe, the client, the text, the time; in the next free migration, T11.9 taking 13), never changed in place (an `UPDATE` trigger, as `recipe_ratings`); the newest per client and recipe is the one shown; a new comment deletes the gymer's earlier comments of that recipe in the same transaction (decision 3: a third recorded exception to keeping every row, after T9.29's read marks and T11.9's daily count, so the rule's sentence is reworded), and "Delete my comment" removes them all; the queries are constant strings with `?` parameters - store tests, `tests/test_store_sql_scan.py`.
- [ ] The coach sees, under each of their recipes in the Recipe library, the newest comment of each client who is connected with them now and shares nutrition now (read from `open_shares`), with the client's display name and no date or time (decision 9); the read goes through `access.authorised_read()` with the nutrition scope and writes one audit row per client whose comment is shown (decision 1), so it is no new exception to auditing; a comment of a client who stopped sharing nutrition or whose relationship ended is not shown (it stays stored, as their rating does, decision 8); the stars stay as T11.8 built them (the average and count from `config.RATING_MIN_RATERS`, never beside a name); no other client ever reads a comment (decision 2) - store tests in the refused-cases matrix (a non-sharing client, an ended relationship, another coach, a deleted recipe), AppTest in English and German.
- [ ] A comment is shown as plain text: Markdown escaped, so a link, an image or HTML in it shows as its characters and loads nothing (as `src/recipes.steps_markdown()` does for the coach's steps); a comment written before the recipe's newest version is marked "written about an earlier version" - AppTest with a comment holding a link, an image, HTML and a heading.
- [ ] Before a gymer saves a comment, the page says in English and German: the coach reads it with your name while you share nutrition with them; your stars stay anonymous, but a comment can show the coach what you rated, also from when the average changes; leave out health details you do not want your coach to have; the comment is not sent to an AI; how to delete it. The gymer sees their own newest comment and can replace or delete it - AppTest in both languages.
- [ ] A comment never reaches a model (decision 5): no prompt, tool, graph state or T11.9's dish workflow reads it, so it does not pass the input guard (decision 4); a scan test fails if the comment table or its read function is used outside `src/store/`, the Recipes page, the Recipe library and `src/store/my_data.py`; a comment is never logged or traced - the scan test, a canary case in `tests/test_observability.py` (save, replace, read by the coach, refuse, delete, export).
- [ ] A gymer's comments are in their export and deleted with their data; they go with the recipe when the coach deletes it and with the coach's data; the coach's export holds no comment, only the audit rows of their reads (decision 8); `my_data.delete_user_data()` and `export_user_data()` name the new table; an edit of the recipe keeps its comments - store tests.
- [ ] Demo mode: the seeded clients Jo and Lee, who rated the first recipe, each get a short made-up comment written by us, with no health detail - review.
- [ ] The record: `.claude/rules/security-privacy.md` ("Personal data" and "The store": the comment, its reader, the audit and the exception to keeping every row), `docs/SECURITY.md` (the known limit grows: a named comment beside an average that moves lets a coach work out a client's stars), `docs/PRD.md` feature 33, "Newly stored" and "Before any real user" (a retention period for comments of an ended relationship; whether free text with health details needs its own consent), AC-48 extended or a new AC; My data's intros and delete note in both languages - review, AppTests in `tests/test_app_my_data.py`.

*Part B: the coach's marks on Today*
- [ ] On the gymer's Today page, an entry logged from a recipe (`meal_items.from_recipe`) shows where it came from: the recipe's name at its newest version (escaped) while the gymer can still read the recipe (their current coach's, `recipes.list_recipes()`), so an edited recipe shows its new name; "From a coach's recipe (no longer available)" when the recipe was deleted, or the relationship with its coach ended (decision 10); an entry logged from the coach's suggested foods (T9.34's `meal_items.from_suggestion`) shows "Suggested by your coach" (decision 10); nothing for an entry with neither mark, or one whose food was changed in an edit (the store drops both marks then, T9.34, T11.8); built by a function in `views/tables.py` (no streamlit), unit-tested without AppTest; AppTests for a kept, an edited and a deleted recipe, after the relationship ended, and for a suggested food, in English and German.
- [ ] Part B changes nothing stored or sent: it reads only `records.list_meal_items()` (which returns the mark already) and `recipes.list_recipes()`; the meal's and the day's totals, My week, the coach's pages and the export stay as they are - AppTest, review.

**Notes:** asked by the owner in T11.9's live check on 2026-11-28: a written comment beside the stars, and where the recipe mark (`from_recipe`) shows. Today no page shows it (nor T9.34's `from_suggestion` flag); it serves only the rating check. Both change what T11.8 stores or shows, so they are a ticket of their own, beyond T11.9. Part B needs no store change and no compliance run on its own; the owner may split it into a ticket of its own (decision 11) and land it first.

**Why T11.8's privacy design matters here.** T11.8 shows the coach only an average and a count, over clients who share nutrition, from 3 raters, never who rated (`src/store/ratings.py`), and records that with few clients a coach may still work a rating out. A comment is free text: it can name the gymer, carry a health detail (Art. 9 data, e.g. "it upset my stomach") and identify them by its style or content even without a name. An anonymous comment would only look anonymous with 3 to 5 clients, so the proposal shows it with the name, where the scope rule and the audit already apply, and says so on the page.

**The owner's decisions (2026-11-28):** the recommended option of each, decision 1 on its own and decisions 2-12 together; the other options stay listed as the record of what was weighed. The criteria above follow them.
1. **Who reads a comment.** (a) The coach, with the client's name, only while the client shares nutrition, through `access.authorised_read()` with an audit row (recommended: honest about what free text gives away, and no new unaudited read); (b) the coach, unnamed, only once `config.RATING_MIN_RATERS` counted clients have rated, not audited like the average (a weak anonymity, a larger known limit); (c) no new table: the comment is sent as a message in the coach-client thread (T11.3), named, in both exports, read also without sharing and after the end, and then reaching models under consents (a) and (b) like any message, T11.5 drafting from it on the gymer's press; (d) a private note only the gymer reads.
2. **Do other clients read comments?** Recommended: never (clients do not know each other, and T11.8 shows a gymer only their own rating).
3. **A replaced comment.** Recommended: deleted in the same transaction as the new one (a third recorded exception to keeping every row; the gymer meant to take the old words back); or kept as history, as replaced ratings are (then a retention period is needed before any real user).
4. **The input guard.** Recommended: none, as the comment reaches no model (the rule: every path to a model passes the guard first); a later ticket that sends comments to a model adds the guard and a consent. Running `guard.check_text()` anyway would refuse fair feedback such as "too salty for my blood pressure".
5. **Sent to a model?** Recommended: never in this ticket (no tool, no T11.9 prompt, no weekly review).
6. **The length limit.** Recommended: 300 characters (`config.RATING_COMMENT_MAX_CHARS`), between a preference (200) and the store's text limit (500).
7. **A comment without stars?** Recommended: no; the comment sits beside a rating, so T11.8's check (the gymer logged the dish) covers it.
8. **When sharing stops or the relationship ends.** Recommended: kept but hidden, as ratings are (and counted again if sharing resumes), deleted with either side's data and with the recipe; or deleted at the end. The coach's export: recommended none, as the comments are the gymer's data and the coach's export keeps only the audit rows.
9. **A date on the coach's view, and a notification.** Recommended: neither: a date or a notification beside an average that just moved would show the coach that client's stars. The coach sees the comments in a fixed order (newest first), with "about an earlier version" when the recipe changed since.
10. **Part B's label for a mark the gymer can no longer read.** Recommended: "From a coach's recipe (no longer available)", with no name, as the store lets a gymer read only their current coach's recipes; keeping the name would need it copied into the meal item at log time (a store change). Also: show T9.34's `from_suggestion` flag the same way ("Suggested by your coach")? Recommended: yes, as it is the same column and no store change.
11. **One ticket or two, the priority and the place in the order.** Recommended: one ticket, P2, after T11.9 has merged (it shares the rating and the migration number); part B may still be split into its own S ticket and land earlier if the owner asks.
12. **Reviews.** A compliance run is needed for part A under `.claude/rules/workflow.md` (personal data stored and shown to another user, the privacy text); `/security-review` is not required (no guard, prompt, tool or secret changes), though the comment's rendering to another user is checked by `code-reviewer`.
