*Part A: a comment beside the stars*
- [ ] A gymer who may rate a recipe (T11.8's check: their current coach's recipe, and they logged its dish) may add a comment in their own words beside their stars, at most `config.RATING_COMMENT_MAX_CHARS` characters (proposed 300), trimmed, on the Recipes page and wherever T11.9 offers the rating; a comment needs a rating of the same recipe first (decision 7); a blank comment is no comment; the store refuses a comment too long or of the wrong type with an error that names the field, never the value, and every case where the gymer may not rate, or has no rating of that recipe yet, with the same `NotFound`, writing nothing - store tests in the refused-cases matrix of `tests/test_store_access.py`.
- [ ] A comment is a row of its own (a new table, e.g. `recipe_comments`: the recipe, the recipe version it was written about, the client, the text, the time; in the next free migration, T11.9 taking 13), never changed in place (an `UPDATE` trigger, as `recipe_ratings`); the newest per client and recipe is the one shown; a new comment deletes the gymer's earlier comments of that recipe in the same transaction (decision 3: a third recorded exception to keeping every row, after T9.29's read marks and T11.9's daily count, so the rule's sentence is reworded), and "Delete my comment" removes them all; after either commit the store checkpoints the write-ahead log (`db.checkpoint()`, as `records.delete_record()` and `recipes.delete_recipe()` do), so the old words' copies leave the `-wal` file; the queries are constant strings with `?` parameters - store tests (a checkpoint after a replace and after a delete), `tests/test_store_sql_scan.py`.
- [ ] The coach sees, under each of their recipes in the Recipe library, the newest comment of each client who is connected with them now and shares nutrition now (read from `open_shares`), with the client's display name and no date or time (decision 9), newest first; the comments are read only while the coach's "Show clients' comments" toggle on the page is on, and each run of the page with it on reads every client connected now who shares nutrition, one at a time, through `access.authorised_read()` with the nutrition scope and the resource type `recipe_comments`, writing one audit row per client read, whether or not that client wrote a comment (decision 1), as T9.30's Notifications page audits on each run; so it is no new exception to auditing, and no unaudited read finds the commenters first; a comment of a client who stopped sharing nutrition or whose relationship ended is not shown (it stays stored, as their rating does, decision 8); the stars stay as T11.8 built them (the average and count from `config.RATING_MIN_RATERS`, never beside a name), but as a comment needs a rating (decision 7), a named comment shows the coach that this client rated the recipe; no other client ever reads a comment (decision 2) - store tests in the refused-cases matrix (a non-sharing client, an ended relationship, another coach, a deleted recipe) and of the audit rows (one per client read), AppTest in English and German (one audit row per client read on each run with the toggle on, none with it off).
- [ ] A comment is shown as plain text: Markdown escaped, so a link, an image or HTML in it shows as its characters and loads nothing (as `src/recipes.steps_markdown()` does for the coach's steps); a comment whose stored recipe version is not the recipe's newest is marked "written about an earlier version" (by the version, never by comparing times, which the store keeps in whole seconds) - AppTest with a comment holding a link, an image, HTML and a heading, and one written before an edit.
- [ ] Before a gymer saves a comment, the page says in English and German: the coach reads it with your name while you share nutrition with them, so the coach sees that you rated this recipe; your stars themselves stay anonymous, but a comment can show the coach how you rated, also when it appears as the average changes; leave out health details you do not want your coach to have; the comment is not sent to an AI; how to delete it. The gymer sees their own newest comment and can replace or delete it - AppTest in both languages.
- [ ] A comment never reaches a model (decision 5): no prompt, tool, graph state or T11.9's dish workflow reads it, so it does not pass the input guard (decision 4); a scan test fails if the comment table or the store's comment functions are named outside `src/store/` (`my_data.py` included), `src/recipes.py` (if it wraps them, as it wraps the rating), `views/recipes.py`, `views/recipe_library.py`, `src/demo.py` (the demo seed's comments, as it seeds the ratings through `recipes.rate_recipe()`) and the page where T11.9 offers the rating (`views/dishes.py` on T11.9's branch, or the file that holds its rating when it merges), and in particular if `src/agent/`, `src/tools/`, `src/prompts/` or a graph state names them; a comment is never logged or traced - the scan test, a canary case in `tests/test_observability.py` (save, replace, read by the coach, refuse, delete, export).
- [ ] A gymer's comments are in their export and deleted with their data; they go with the recipe when the coach deletes it and with the coach's data; the coach's export holds no comment, only the audit rows of their reads (decision 8); `my_data.delete_user_data()` and `export_user_data()` name the new table; an edit of the recipe keeps its comments - store tests.
- [ ] Demo mode: the seeded clients Jo and Lee, who rated the first recipe, each get a short made-up comment written by us, with no health detail - review.
- [ ] The record: `.claude/rules/security-privacy.md` ("Personal data" and "The store": the comment, its reader, the audit and the exception to keeping every row), `docs/SECURITY.md` (the known limit grows: a named comment shows the coach that its client rated, and a new comment at the top of the list, as the average moves, lets a coach work out that client's stars), `docs/PRD.md` feature 33 and "Newly stored" (each "never shown who rated" reworded: the coach never sees a client's stars, but sees who commented, and so rated), and "Before any real user" (a retention period for comments of an ended relationship; whether free text with health details needs its own consent), with AC-48 extended or a new AC; the texts and docstrings that say the coach never sees who rated, or sees only the average, reworded the same way: the Recipe library's intro and the rating note on the gymer's page (`recipes.rating_counts_note`), both in `src/i18n/recipes.py`, the coach's My data intro in `src/i18n/account.py` ("only the average shows on your Recipe library"), each in English and German, and the docstrings of `src/store/ratings.py` and `views/recipe_library.py`; My data's intros and delete note in both languages - review, AppTests of the reworded texts and in `tests/test_app_my_data.py`.

**Why T11.8's privacy design matters here.** T11.8 shows the coach only an average and a count, over clients who share nutrition, from 3 raters, never who rated (`src/store/ratings.py`), and records that with few clients a coach may still work a rating out. A comment is free text: it can name the gymer, carry a health detail (Art. 9 data, e.g. "it upset my stomach") and identify them by its style or content even without a name. An anonymous comment would only look anonymous with 3 to 5 clients, so the proposal shows it with the name, where the scope rule and the audit already apply, and says so on the page.

**The owner's decisions (2026-11-28):** the recommended option of each, decision 1 on its own and decisions 2-12 together; the other options stay listed as the record of what was weighed. The criteria above follow them. Code review round 1 of NMH1988/fitkompass#63 (2026-11-28) made seven criteria exact with the same aim, which the owner asked for the same day ("fix all"): a named comment shows the coach who rated, so the texts that promise otherwise are reworded and the gymer's page says so; the audit is one row per client read on each run while a toggle shows the comments (an unaudited read to find the commenters would be the exception decision 1 rules out); the scan test's list of files names T11.9's rating page and `src/recipes.py`; the suggestion flag names no coach, so "your coach" only for food logged since the current relationship started; the claims about `from_suggestion` corrected (it feeds the coach's weekly share, and is a column of its own); a checkpoint after a comment is replaced or deleted; Today's recipe name as a table cell, not escaped; and, as nits, the recipe version stored with a comment and the `NotFound` for a comment with no rating. Code review round 2 (the same day) found three gaps the fixes left, taken the same way: part B also reads the gymer's own relationships (for the suggestion label's start), the scan test allows `src/demo.py` (the demo seeds the comments as it seeds the ratings), and the owner's approval of the change is recorded before merging; as nits, the toggle's audit is an AppTest, the texts to reword name `recipes.rating_counts_note` and the coach's My data intro, and part B tests a suggested food after the relationship ended with no new coach. A new comment at the top of the coach's list, as the average moves, is covered by the known limit the record criterion adds to `docs/SECURITY.md` (the reviewer's question; decision 9).
1. **Who reads a comment.** (a) The coach, with the client's name, only while the client shares nutrition, through `access.authorised_read()` with an audit row (recommended: honest about what free text gives away, and no new unaudited read); (b) the coach, unnamed, only once `config.RATING_MIN_RATERS` counted clients have rated, not audited like the average (a weak anonymity, a larger known limit); (c) no new table: the comment is sent as a message in the coach-client thread (T11.3), named, in both exports, read also without sharing and after the end, and then reaching models under consents (a) and (b) like any message, T11.5 drafting from it on the gymer's press; (d) a private note only the gymer reads.
2. **Do other clients read comments?** Recommended: never (clients do not know each other, and T11.8 shows a gymer only their own rating).
3. **A replaced comment.** Recommended: deleted in the same transaction as the new one (a third recorded exception to keeping every row; the gymer meant to take the old words back); or kept as history, as replaced ratings are (then a retention period is needed before any real user).
4. **The input guard.** Recommended: none, as the comment reaches no model (the rule: every path to a model passes the guard first); a later ticket that sends comments to a model adds the guard and a consent. Running `guard.check_text()` anyway would refuse fair feedback such as "too salty for my blood pressure".
5. **Sent to a model?** Recommended: never in this ticket (no tool, no T11.9 prompt, no weekly review).
6. **The length limit.** Recommended: 300 characters (`config.RATING_COMMENT_MAX_CHARS`), between a preference (200) and the store's text limit (500).
7. **A comment without stars?** Recommended: no; the comment sits beside a rating, so T11.8's check (the gymer logged the dish) covers it.
8. **When sharing stops or the relationship ends.** Recommended: kept but hidden, as ratings are (and counted again if sharing resumes), deleted with either side's data and with the recipe; or deleted at the end. The coach's export: recommended none, as the comments are the gymer's data and the coach's export keeps only the audit rows.
9. **A date on the coach's view, and a notification.** Recommended: neither: a date or a notification beside an average that just moved would show the coach that client's stars. The coach sees the comments in a fixed order (newest first), with "about an earlier version" when the recipe changed since.
10. **Part B's label for a mark the gymer can no longer read.** Recommended: "From a coach's recipe (no longer available)", with no name, as the store lets a gymer read only their current coach's recipes; keeping the name would need it copied into the meal item at log time (a store change). Also: show T9.34's `from_suggestion` flag the same way ("Suggested by your coach")? Recommended: yes, as the store already returns it with each entry, so no store change.
11. **One ticket or two, the priority and the place in the order.** Recommended: one ticket, P2, after T11.9 has merged (it shares the rating and the migration number); part B may still be split into its own S ticket and land earlier if the owner asks.
12. **Reviews.** A compliance run is needed for part A under `.claude/rules/workflow.md` (personal data stored and shown to another user, the privacy text); `/security-review` is not required (no guard, prompt, tool or secret changes), though the comment's rendering to another user is checked by `code-reviewer`.
